German letters are a different sport

I have lived in Germany for over 12 years. My German is fine for ordering coffee and arguing about the Deutsche Bahn. A formal letter is something else.

You need the right greeting, the right verb, the right tone. A wrong word in a letter to your landlord or the Ausländerbehörde can cost real money or real time. So most of us do the same thing:

  • Paste an English draft into a translator
  • Hope it does not sound rude
  • Ask a German friend (again)
  • Send it and worry for a week

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. These are general templates and general information. German rules and deadlines depend on your situation, your contract and your state. For important matters (your home, your job, your residence permit), check with a Mieterverein, the Verbraucherzentrale, a counselling centre or a lawyer before you send anything.

Do I have to speak German?

No. Every letter has an English translation, so you know exactly what you are sending.

How up to date is it?

Last updated October 2026. Each template shows the main deadline or rule to check before you send.

Does it work on my phone?

The PDF opens on any device. The yellow fill in boxes work best on a computer (Adobe Reader, Chrome, Edge, Mac Preview). On a phone, copy the German text from the PDF and paste it into your email.

Can I send these as emails?

Many of them, yes: sick notice, appointment request, vacation. Some must be on paper with a signature, such as resigning from a job or ending a lease. The Watch out box in each template tells you which.

Does it cover my state or city?

The templates are for all of Germany. Some rules differ by state, for example school leave. Where that matters, the Watch out box says so.

I am on a visa or applying for citizenship. Will this help?

The kit includes letters for the Ausländerbehörde and other authorities: appointment requests, deadline extensions and appeals. It does not explain immigration law. For that, see the free citizenship checklist.
