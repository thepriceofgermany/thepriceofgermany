German cost of living calculator

Pick two German cities and see how much each one really costs to live in. You will get a real, category-by-category breakdown: rent, groceries, transport, utilities, and estimated take-home pay on a German salary.

Most cost-of-living comparisons give you one vague number and call it a day. This one does not. It breaks a German city down the way you would actually feel it: what you pay for a one-bedroom, what a full grocery cart costs, what getting around runs, and what the lights and heating add up to. The headline number is shareable, but the categories underneath it are where the real story lives, and they are where one German city pulls ahead of another.

Entered as euros. German income tax and social insurance are national, so take-home pay is the same in every city.

Context notes

  • Figures are monthly unless stated, in euros.
  • These are informed estimates anchored to published indices and city rent data, not invoices. Berlin last checked 2026-08, Munich last checked 2026-08.
  • German public health insurance is about 14.6 percent of gross income, split with your employer, and already deducted before your net pay lands. It does not change from city to city.
  • Net-pay figures use a simplified single-filer 2026 model and will differ from your real payslip. Always run a real offer through a proper calculator before you accept it.

How these numbers are estimated

Every figure here is an informed estimate, not an invoice. We anchor each category to published cost-of-living indices and to real city rent data, then review the outliers by hand so a cheap city reads cheap and an expensive one reads expensive. All figures are in euros. Each city carries the month it was last checked, shown in the context notes, because prices go stale.

The take-home pay figures use a simplified 2026 single-filer tax model: German income tax plus the solidarity surcharge plus the mandatory social-insurance contributions. Income tax and social insurance are set nationally, so take-home pay does not change from city to city. The figures will not match your exact payslip, so treat them as a realistic ballpark and always run a real offer through a proper calculator before you accept it.
