Healthcare

The structural difference matters more than the number. In the US this is a separate, variable cost outside your paycheck: a premium plus a deductible when you actually get sick. In Germany public health insurance is about 14.6 percent of gross income, split between you and your employer, and it is already taken out before your net pay lands.

Context notes

  • These are informed estimates anchored to published indices and city rent data, not invoices. New York last checked 2026-08, Berlin last checked 2026-08.
  • German public health insurance bundles things Americans often pay extra for: no separate premium bill, no deductible, and dependents are covered on one contribution.
  • Net-pay figures use a simplified single-filer 2026 model and will differ from your real payslip. Always run a real offer through a proper calculator before you accept it.

How these numbers are estimated

Every figure here is an informed estimate, not an invoice. We anchor each category to published cost-of-living indices and to real city rent data, then review the outliers by hand so a cheap city reads cheap and an expensive one reads expensive. US figures are in dollars, German figures in euros, and the headline converts between them at a fixed rate set on the data date. Each city carries the month it was last checked, shown in the context notes, because prices go stale.

The take-home pay figures use a simplified 2026 single-filer tax model: US federal brackets plus FICA plus an effective state and local rate, and German income tax plus the solidarity surcharge plus the mandatory social-insurance contributions. They will not match your exact payslip, so treat them as a realistic ballpark and always run a real offer through a proper calculator before you accept it.
